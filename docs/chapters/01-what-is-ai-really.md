title: What Is AI, Really? (A Friend's Version)
summary: In plain English, with one exercise you can finish before your tea goes cold.

## What you'll learn

By the end of this chapter you'll be able to:

- Explain what an AI "chat" actually is — in one sentence, to a friend
- Try your first AI conversation without fear or fuss
- Spot the one mistake everyone makes with AI answers

## Start with the why

Picture a very well-read friend who has skimmed most of the internet. Ask
them a question and they'll answer fast, in mostly the right ballpark.
That's a chat with an AI. It is not magic, and it is not a person —
it's a pattern machine that wants to be helpful.

This matters because understanding *that* changes how you use it: you stop
expecting perfection and start asking better questions.

## Walk it through, step by step

1. Open any AI chat tool you like (a free one is fine to start).
2. Ask it something you'd ask a patient, curious friend:
   "Explain what a computer virus is, like I've never heard of one."
3. Read the answer. Notice its voice — clear? friendly? too long?
4. Follow up! Try: "Shorter, please. And give me one real-life example."
5. Save whichever answer you actually liked — that's your first AI gold.

Behind the scenes, your question and its answer look like ordinary text:

```text
You:    Explain what a computer virus is, like I've never heard of one.
AI:     [a short, friendly explanation appears here]
You:    Shorter, please. And give me one real-life example.
```

## Watch out for this

The classic beginner mistake: *believing every answer completely*.
AI sometimes sounds confident when it's wrong — nurses call this "sounding
well". The friendly fix: ask it "How sure are you, and what should I check
to confirm?" Doubt is a habit, not an insult.

## Try it yourself (worksheet prompt)

Open your worksheet for this chapter and write down one ordinary question
you'd like answered this week. Small wins > big tests.

## In plain English

AI is a 'well-read' pattern machine, not a mind-reader. Ask like you're
talking to a friend, double-check the important stuff, and iterate — the
conversation is the tool.
