## How to use the glossary

When a chapter uses a term that might scare a beginner, the author adds it
below. Keep entries short, warm, and jargon-free. If your definition needs
another definition — simplify, don't cascade.

## Words in this edition

**AI (artificial intelligence)**
Software that finds patterns in lots of examples and uses them to answer,
write, or predict. A well-read pattern machine, not a mind.

**Chat**
A back-and-forth text conversation with an AI tool. You type; it answers.

**Prompt**
The words you type to ask for something. A good prompt is a clear request
with a little context: role, task, and format if you care.

**Hallucination**
When an AI sounds confident but the facts aren't right. Check anything
important — same habit as double-checking medication you haven't seen before.

**Open source**
Software anyone can download, inspect, and change. Good for privacy,
great for learning.

**Local AI**
Running AI on your own computer so your words never leave the house.

**MCP (Model Context Protocol)**
A way to give AI tools extra abilities — like handing your friend a
toolbox before they start the job.

**Small win**
One tiny task finished. The Asdale way is built entirely out of these.
